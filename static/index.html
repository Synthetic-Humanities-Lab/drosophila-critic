METHOD / What is this fly, exactly?

A fly listens. We inspect the traces.

The recording becomes sound data. A published model of antennal mechanics transforms that sound; its vibration envelope stimulates the same 138 hearing neurons used by fly.ai. The MaleCNS connectome stays fixed. No words, meaning, or language model enter this process.

The antenna-to-neuron connection is an engineering assumption. This is a literature-informed hearing model coupled to a simulated nervous system, not a validated prediction of a real fly listening to speech.

Selected nerve cells supply walking, turning, and flight commands. Separate, pretrained flybody controllers coordinate the legs and wings in MuJoCo physics. Our adapter, takeoff and landing rules, and arena steering are additional modeling choices. The fly can move in silence too. These movements are model outputs, not a score for the poem or a prediction of a living fly’s behaviour.

RESPONSE means what the simulation did. Any interpretation is an inference from those measurements; it does not reveal feelings or understanding.

Reading the display


Use the 2D map instead

Amber points mark recorded spikes in a fixed sample of 12,000 neurons, at their supplied coordinates. For the examples, that spatial view shows one run; the traces show the average and range of four runs. Visitor results use one run and its own silence control.

The grey trace is the same model run with no sound, for the same duration and starting conditions. The two recordings have different lengths. Their levels are matched with linear gain; pauses and dynamics remain. Visitor recordings use the same target level, lowered if needed to avoid clipping.

The antenna trace shows modeled vibration strength in 20 ms frames, not a resolved animation of every oscillation. The waveform and input traces use separate scales, shared across recordings. Antennal colour follows that modeled strength. Reader gestures are theatrical.
